Tribute to the Women of the 9 August March

Women marching for freedom and equality on 9 August 1956.
On 9 August 1956, an estimated 20,000 women marched to the Union Buildings in Pretoria, South Africa, to protest against the oppressive pass laws imposed by the apartheid government. This historic event marked a significant moment in the anti-apartheid struggle, showcasing the united strength of women from diverse backgrounds. The women involved in the march risked their safety and livelihoods, demonstrating unyielding courage in the face of adversity. Leaders such as Lillian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph,Rahima Moosa, and Frances Baard were at the forefront of this movement. Their relentless pursuit of justice and equality not only led to notable changes in South Africa but also inspired future generations to continue the fight for human rights and gender equality.
